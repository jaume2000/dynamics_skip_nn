\begin{abstract}
Residual blocks compute $x + f(x)$, the form of one explicit Euler step.
We refine trained residual stages at inference by applying each block $R$ times with
step $1/R$, which keeps the integration horizon and needs no retraining.
Refined $128$-fold, the stages of ConvNeXt, Swin and ResNet lose less than
\refineLossBound{} ImageNet top-1 point, whereas changing the horizon collapses every
network.
The refined path shows that a trained stage is not one ODE but a composition of one
ODE per block.
In ConvNeXt-T the field switches at every block boundary, where the heading turns by
about $\turnMean^\circ$, and each of the nine blocks writes a direction nearly
orthogonal to the previous ones (consecutive cosine $\cosUntied$, against
$\cosRandom$ for random blocks of matched strength) that no later block reverses.
Sharing one set of weights across the nine blocks turns the stage into a single
autonomous flow: it is straighter ($\strTied$ against $\strUntied$), has no switches
and is read by its own frozen decoder much earlier, with $\prefixTiedThree\%$ top-1
after three of nine steps against $\prefixUntiedThree\%$ for the standard stage.
Sharing costs $\accGapTied$ points with $\paramsTiedFracFewer\%$ fewer parameters, and
tripling the number of shared steps reaches only $\accTiedDTwentySeven\%$, recovering
none of the gap, which suggests that the gap is the price of a single field rather
than of depth.

\end{abstract}

Residual blocks have been considered ODE sovers for long since they compute a composition of
$x + f(x)$, which is the form of one explicit Euler step.
We refine trained residual stages at inference by applying each block $R$ times with
step $1/R$, which keeps the integration horizon and needs no retraining.
The refined path shows that a trained stage is not one ODE but a composition of one
ODE per block. Therefore, we can interpret these networks as a composition of ODEs solved by
one Euler step.
Nonetheless, we used this technique to gather better quality of the feature trajectories inside the
network to study its dynamics compared agains a weight-tied Convnext-T.
Without one third of the original model's params, it performed very similarly with 80.34\5 top-1 ImageNet-1k
compared with our Convnext-T with 81\%.
The dynamics of both are the opposite. The trajectory of the orginal model has to compute a trajectory two times
greater compared to the straigt line directly to the final feature with 0.5 of straightness, the weight-tied one
performed 0.78\% straigtness. Normal residual blocks use teir params only once, therefore they learn to
compute simple curves where they push the features onto very different directions, all of them towards the goal,
but never all of them to the best direction, cancelling partially the direciton previous layer choosed. Weight-tied
models don't show this problem, but perform a little bit lower. So we created an hibrid aproach where we define
a function composed by two sets of params that are added: first one consist on shared params, the second one
custom params for each block and both of them have weight decay, therefore, the shared param can be interpreted as
an autonomous field and the second set is the non-autonomos field, performing better than both networks with a 81.1\% top-1 accuracy.
