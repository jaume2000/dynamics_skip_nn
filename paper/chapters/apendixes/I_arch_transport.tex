\section{Transport coherence in other architectures}
\label{app:arch-transport}

We compute the Gram statistics of \cref{sec:coherence} at native
resolution ($R{=}1$) on the same $500$ images for stage~3 of the torchvision Swin-T, ResNet-50 and
ResNet-101 and for random stages with Xavier weights at gain $1$ and at the
gain $g^\ast$ that matches the trained field strength (\cref{app:random-init}).
Swin is reported with all tokens and without the four corner tokens
(\cref{app:massive-activations}).
ResNet stages exclude the first block, which has a projection shortcut, and the field
$f_i$ includes the ReLU applied after the sum, so $n{=}5$ and $n{=}22$.
Nine, six or twenty-two orthogonal increments of equal length would give a
straightness of $1/\sqrt n$ (last column).

Swin-T stage~3 is a coherent transport: straightness $0.80$, consecutive cosine $0.43$
and positive survival for every image and block.
Without the corner tokens it is still well above its random baseline ($0.67$ against
$0.42$), so the coherence is not only the growth of the corner tokens along their own
direction.
Its random baseline sits at $1/\sqrt 6$, as for ConvNeXt.

Both ResNets go the other way and are the only architectures here with genuine
reversal: opposed fraction $E$ is $0.19$ (ResNet-50) and $0.24$ (ResNet-101), against
about $0.07$--$0.09$ for their random baselines, while ConvNeXt and Swin have $E{=}0$.
Trained ResNet-50 is \emph{less} straight than its random baseline ($0.37$ against
$0.43$), with negative consecutive cosine ($-0.11$) and negative survival for every
image and block; ResNet-101 behaves similarly ($0.16$ against $0.22$ to $0.23$).
Random ResNet stages already show negative survival, unlike random ConvNeXt stages.
One candidate is the ReLU after the sum, which makes $f_i$ include a rectification of
$x+F_i(x)$ that can remove part of $x$; we have not tested it, and we report the ResNet
statistics as a fact about these checkpoints rather than as a mechanism.
