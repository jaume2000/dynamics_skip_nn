\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Residual blocks can be refined indefinitely at inference: as long as the
integration horizon $T$ is kept, ConvNeXt, Swin and ResNet stages converge to a
continuous limit that loses less than one top-1 point, whereas breaking $T$
collapses every network.
Refinement turns a residual stage into a trajectory, and that trajectory
separates two regimes behind the same algebra $x+f(x)$.
A single shared block, reused over the whole stage, is an autonomous transport:
it searches for a heading, expands along it and refines, and its features become
usable by the decoder early.
Nine independent blocks form a switched transport: shallow arcs joined by sharp
turns at every block boundary, where almost half of the motion is undone.
This redundancy is why one shared block recovers almost all of the accuracy of
the untied stage ($-0.6$ points with $33\%$ fewer parameters).
The same probe applies to any residual stream, and suggests a design goal:
fields that stay mostly autonomous, so that every parameter pushes toward the
same target.
