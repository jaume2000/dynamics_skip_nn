\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:rel_work}

\paragraph{Residual networks as discretised ODEs.}
A residual update $x+f(x)$~\citep{he2016resnet} is one explicit Euler step of an
ODE~\citep{e2017proposal,haber2017stable,lu2018beyond}, a reading that led to
Neural ODEs~\citep{chen2019neuralordinarydifferentialequations}, multi-level training
schemes~\citep{chang2018multilevel} and continuous-in-depth
architectures~\citep{queiruga2020continuousindepthneuralnetworks}.
Whether a trained ResNet actually behaves as such a discretisation is debated.
\citet{queiruga2020continuousindepthneuralnetworks} find that standard ResNets fail
to act as meaningful integrators beyond the syntactic analogy;
\citet{sander2022residualneuralnetworksdiscretize} show that the gap between a ResNet
and its Neural ODE need not vanish with depth unless the residual maps vary smoothly
in depth;
\citet{cohen2021scaling} find that, depending on the architecture and on how trained
weights scale with depth, the deep limit can be an ODE, a stochastic differential
equation, or neither; and \citet{marion2024implicit} prove that, under gradient flow and
in the large-depth limit, a ResNet initialised as a Neural ODE discretisation remains
one through training.
These works either train continuous models or study the limit of growing depth.
We instead refine \emph{already trained}, standard networks at inference, keeping the
integration horizon fixed, and use the refined path as a probe rather than as a model.

\paragraph{Weight sharing and looping in depth.}
Sharing one residual block across depth links ResNets to recurrent
networks~\citep{liao2016bridging}; sharing convolutions across the blocks of a stage
yields compact CNNs~\citep{sharesnet}, as does soft parameter sharing across
layers~\citep{savarese2019learning}.
In transformers, universal and looped transformers reuse a single layer or a fixed
block of layers in a loop~\citep{dehghani2019universal,giannou2023looped}, ALBERT shares all
layers~\citep{lan2020albert}, and partial sharing schemes trade parameters against
accuracy~\citep{takase2021lessons,bae2024relaxed}.
Iterating a shared block beyond its training depth is fragile: recurrent networks
``overthink'' and degrade unless they recall the input and are trained with a randomly
sampled number of iterations~\citep{bansal2022endtoend}; recurrent-depth language models
that inject the input at every step and sample the iteration count during training
remain stable far beyond their typical depth~\citep{geiping2025recurrent}; and deep
equilibrium models replace the iteration by a fixed point~\citep{bai2019deep}.
For us weight tying is not a compression method but a control: it turns a switched
stack of fields into a single autonomous one, and its trajectory explains both why a
shared stage is nearly as accurate and why integrating past the trained horizon fails.

\paragraph{Redundancy and iterative refinement across depth.}
Residual networks are robust to dropping or reordering blocks: they behave like
ensembles of shallow paths~\citep{veit2016residualnetworksbehavelike}, can be trained
with stochastic depth~\citep{huang2016stochastic}, and have been read as iterative
estimators that refine one representation~\citep{greff2017highway,jastrzebski2018iterative}.
Language models show a similar tolerance: middle layers can be skipped, swapped or
run in parallel with graceful degradation, although repeating a single middle layer is
harmful~\citep{sun2025painters,lad2025robustness}, and many deep layers can be pruned
with a small amount of healing fine-tuning~\citep{gromov2024unreasonable}.
Intermediate states are commonly decoded with linear probes~\citep{alain2016probes} or
lenses~\citep{belrose2023tuned}.
These studies establish that depth is redundant; we measure \emph{where} the redundancy
sits, as motion that consecutive blocks undo, concentrated at block boundaries, and
decode prefixes with the frozen downstream network rather than a trained probe.

\paragraph{Geometry of representations along depth.}
Under the manifold hypothesis, deep networks gradually untangle and flatten the data
manifold~\citep{bengio2013representation,dicarlo2007untangling,brahma2016why,
hauser2017principles,naitzat2020topology,cohen2020separability}, and its intrinsic
dimension changes systematically with depth~\citep{ansuini2019intrinsic,valeriani2023geometry}.
Closer to our measurements, the curvature of representation trajectories decreases in
human perception of natural videos~\citep{henaff2019perceptual} and from early to
middle layers of language models~\citep{hosseini2023straighten}.
Recent work treats the residual stream of transformers as a dynamical system whose
velocity and acceleration change across layers and whose trajectories are
curved~\citep{fernando2025transformerdynamics}, and measures trajectory length and
turning-angle curvature across
layers~\citep{pandey2026trajectorygeometrytransformerrepresentations}.
Those analyses sample one point per layer.
Refinement provides hundreds of points per block, so velocity, angular velocity, the
tangential/normal split of acceleration and curvature can be estimated within blocks
and separated from the jumps between them.
Finally, outlier features---massive activations in language
models~\citep{sun2024massiveactivationslargelanguage,pmlr-v306-sun26d} and high-norm
tokens in vision transformers~\citep{darcet2024vision}---dominate such statistics; we
find their analogues in ConvNeXt and Swin and exclude them from the kinematics.
