\section{Refining Residual Stages}
\label{sec:method}

We read a residual vision network as a three-part map: stem, stage~1 and stage~2
encode the image into a latent state $x_0$; the deepest residual stage transports
$x_0$ to a state $x_T$; stage~4 and the head decode $x_T$ into logits.
We study that middle transport, stage~3 of ConvNeXt~\citep{liu2022convnext},
Swin~\citep{liu2021swin} and ResNet~\citep{he2016resnet}.
It holds most of the residual depth (nine of the eighteen blocks of ConvNeXt-T),
keeps one representation and spatial resolution and has enough blocks to study
dynamics.
We take its $n$ blocks as the trained horizon $T{=}n$ and refine the discretisation
with $R$ repetitions of step $\Delta t{=}1/R$ (\cref{sec:indefinite_looping}).
Per-block step sizes are absorbed into the learned weights, so integrating each block
over one unit of depth matches the step the model has internalised.

Refinement converges for any Lipschitz residual field, trained or random
(\cref{app:random-init}), so the question is what the limit keeps and how the path
is organised.
A weight-tied stage~3, one shared field reused over $T{=}n$, gives a clean autonomous
ODE (\cref{sec:weight_tied}); the standard stage is a switched composition of $n$
smooth pieces, hence the jumps in $\|f\|$ at block boundaries in \cref{fig:teaser}.
Refining both at inference yields high-resolution trajectories from $x_0$ to $x_T$,
which we compare kinematically (\cref{sec:geometrical_analysis}) and with
basis-independent statistics of the block increments (\cref{sec:coherence}).
