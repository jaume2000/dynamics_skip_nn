Residual connections~\citep{he2016resnet} made very deep networks trainable by
letting each block add a correction to its input, $x\mapsto x+f(x)$.
Because every block writes into the same representation, a stage can be read as one
state moving through feature space, and the update has the form of one explicit
Euler step of an ODE~\citep{e2017proposal,haber2017stable,lu2018beyond}.
That reading inspired Neural ODEs~\citep{chen2019neuralordinarydifferentialequations},
but a stack approaches an ODE only if its blocks vary smoothly in
depth~\citep{sander2022residualneuralnetworksdiscretize}.
Trained vision networks do not: the residual norm of an untied stage jumps at every
block boundary (\cref{fig:teaser}, bottom right), and interpolating the weights of
consecutive blocks costs $6.6$ to $23.3$ top-1 points (\cref{app:bilinear}).
Each block is a smooth field on its own unit of depth, and the stage is a
\emph{switched} flow that changes field at every integer.

Two questions follow.
The first is geometric: nine blocks give nine states, too few to tell a smooth
transport from a sequence of unrelated jumps.
The second is about parameters: a ConvNeXt-T whose nine stage-3 blocks share one set
of weights reaches $80.34\%$ ImageNet top-1, $0.62$ points below the untied model with
$33\%$ fewer parameters (\cref{sec:weight_tied}).
What do nine independent blocks do that one shared field does not?

We address both with one inference-time probe.
We read each block as a field integrated over one unit of depth and refine it: apply it
$R$ times with step $\Delta t{=}1/R$, so that the stage horizon $T$ is kept, without
any fine-tuning.
Convergence is guaranteed for any Lipschitz field, trained or random
(\cref{app:random-init}), so it is not the result; it is what turns each stage into a
trajectory with hundreds of states per block.
On ConvNeXt, Swin and ResNet the limit costs less than one top-1 point, whereas
changing $T$ collapses every network (\cref{sec:indefinite_looping}).

The trajectories separate two regimes behind the same algebra (\cref{fig:teaser}).
The weight-tied stage is an autonomous flow $\dot x=f(x)$ that first searches for a
heading, then expands along it and finally refines at low speed.
The untied stage is a switched flow $\dot x=f_{\lfloor t\rfloor}(x)$: nine shallow
arcs joined by turns of about $80^\circ$ at every block boundary, where $45\%$ of the
coordinates of the velocity change sign against $0.2\%$ per step inside a block
(\cref{sec:geometrical_analysis}).
Basis-independent statistics of the block increments show what the switches do
(\cref{sec:coherence}): each untied block writes in a direction nearly orthogonal to the previous one, but
positively correlated with it (cosine $0.17$, against $0.00$ for random blocks of
matched strength), and no later block erases it.
The stage advances as a staircase whose straightness, $0.50$, sits between that of
random blocks ($0.33$) and of the shared flow ($0.78$); the detour is made at the
switches, since the arc length exceeds the sum of block chords by only $1.5\%$.
A frozen decoder also reads the shared state earlier in the stage; why the coherent
field nearly matches the staircase remains open (\cref{sec:discussion}).

Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Refinement at fixed horizon} as a training-free probe whose limit
    stays usable on ConvNeXt, Swin and ResNet (\cref{sec:indefinite_looping}).
    \item \textbf{Autonomous versus switched transport}: a weight-tied stage as an
    autonomous control for the standard stack, close in accuracy and different in
    kinematics (\cref{sec:weight_tied,sec:geometrical_analysis}).
    \item \textbf{A basis-independent measure of transport coherence}: straightness
    split into a between-block and a within-block part, alignment between blocks and
    with the destination, survival of each block's increment, a random baseline of matched strength and prefix decoding with the frozen downstream network
    (\cref{sec:coherence}).
\end{itemize}
