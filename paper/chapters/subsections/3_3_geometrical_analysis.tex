\subsection{Geometrical analysis of feature trajectories}
\label{sec:geometrical_analysis}

We now compare the geometry of stage-3 trajectories under a single shared field
versus nine independent blocks.
The weight-tied ConvNeXt traces a smooth three-phase arc over $T{=}9$.
The untied ConvNeXt-T---and likewise ResNet and Swin---does not: each block
learns a one-step map, so the refined path is a polyline with a new heading at
every integer.

\subsubsection{Massive activations excluded from analysis}
\label{sec:massive_activations}

While inspecting the trajectories obtained with our first approach, we noticed that those of ConvNeXt and Swin were unusually homogeneous. Analysing the features revealed massive activations \citep{sun2024massiveactivationslargelanguage}. In both the shared and the untied ConvNeXt a single stage-3 channel exceeds all others by more than an order of magnitude and keeps growing in magnitude along the stage, a channel-concentrated pattern also reported for diffusion transformers \citep{gan2025ditf}. In Swin the outliers are instead the four corner tokens, whose norms end the stage more than an order of magnitude above those of typical positions, reminiscent of the high-norm tokens of vision transformers \citep{darcet2024vision}. ResNet stage~3 shows no comparable outlier. This is consistent with \citet{pmlr-v306-sun26d}, who find that massive activations arise in pre-norm residual blocks and are suppressed when the vector-wise normalisation is replaced by an element-wise one: ConvNeXt and Swin normalise each position across channels with LayerNorm, whereas BatchNorm~\citep{ioffe2015batchnorm} in ResNet normalises each channel independently. Details and figures for all models are given in \cref{app:massive-activations}.

The trained ConvNeXt relies on this channel: zeroing it after every residual step of the shared model reduces ImageNet top-1 from $80.3\%$ to $67.8\%$, whereas zeroing a random stage-3 channel costs at most $0.3$~pp. This does not make the channel necessary, since zeroing it also changes the LayerNorm statistics of every position, and in LLMs massive activations can be removed through architectural changes without loss of performance \citep{pmlr-v306-sun26d}.

Unlike in LLMs, where massive activations are injected by a few early blocks and then stay nearly constant until late blocks cancel them \citep{pmlr-v306-sun26d}, the ConvNeXt channel evolves throughout the stage with the same kinematic profile as the rest of the state (\cref{fig:massive-phase}). In LLMs these activations act as fixed, input-independent bias terms, i.e.\ implicit parameters of the network \citep{sun2024massiveactivationslargelanguage,pmlr-v306-sun26d}, but in the shared model the explicit parameters are identical at every step, so the blocks can only access depth through the state itself. We conjecture that this channel provides that signal, in the same way that an autonomous system can emulate a non-autonomous one by carrying time as an extra state variable \citep{strogatz2015nonlinear}, which time-dependent Neural ODEs implement explicitly as an extra input channel \citep{dupont2019augmented}. Our observations are consistent with this reading but do not establish it; a dedicated study comparing the dynamics with and without the channel is left for future work.

\subsubsection{Trajectories of weight-tied ConvNeXt}
\label{sec:tied-trajectory-shape}
We report the same four kinematics on every model, ignoring the massive ConvNeXt
channel and the massive Swin tokens (ResNet has neither).
We measure velocity $\|v\|$, angular velocity $\omega$, the tangential/normal split
$(a_t,a_n)$ of acceleration, and curvature $\kappa{=}a_n/\|v\|^2$
(\cref{fig:kinematics-compare}; $N{=}500$).
A turning-angle curvature between consecutive layers has been used for language
models~\citep{pandey2026trajectorygeometrytransformerrepresentations}, and curvature of
representation trajectories has been used to show that human visual
perception~\citep{henaff2019perceptual} and language models~\citep{hosseini2023straighten}
straighten their inputs; there it is measured across the input sequence (frames or
tokens), here along depth for a fixed input.
Speed says how fast the state moves; $\omega$ and $\kappa$ say how much its
heading bends.
Here $\kappa=\mathrm{d}\theta/\mathrm{d}s$ is Frenet curvature of the heading of
$v$: $\kappa=0$ is a straight path, $\kappa\to\infty$ a corner.
The number is in $1/\mathrm{length}$, so it is read against the arc travelled
per unit depth, $s\approx\|v\|\Delta t$.
Then $\Delta\theta\approx\kappa\|v\|\Delta t$ (equivalently $\omega\Delta t$).
A value of order $1$ with $\|v\|$ of tens would be a hairpin per unit of depth;
$\kappa\sim 10^{-2}$ still turns the heading by tens of degrees over a block.

    massive channel ignored; mean and P5--P95 over $N{=}500$).
    \textbf{Top:} weight-tied ConvNeXt.
    \textbf{Bottom:} untied ConvNeXt-T.
    Columns: velocity $\|v\|$, angular velocity $\omega$, acceleration
    decomposition $(\|a\|,a_t,a_n)$, curvature $\kappa{=}a_n/\|v\|^2$.
    The shared field traces a smooth three-phase arc; the untied stack is

    \label{fig:kinematics-compare}

Read together, the shared curves in \cref{fig:kinematics-compare}~(top) are a
single arc with three phases, not a catalogue of independent plots.

Early on, $\|v\|$ is still moderate while $\omega$ and $\kappa$ peak
($\omega{\approx}0.70\,\mathrm{rad}/t$, $\kappa{\approx}1.4{\times}10^{-2}$).
Per unit of depth that is $\Delta\theta\approx\kappa\|v\|\approx 0.7\,\mathrm{rad}$
($\approx 40^\circ$): small steps that keep changing direction, as if committing
to an axis.

Through the first half of the stage that axis is used.
The signed tangential acceleration $a_t=\langle\hat v,a\rangle$ turns
positive, peaks near $t{\approx}2.3$, and $\|v\|$ follows a bell, as in
\cref{fig:refinement}~(right) and \cref{fig:teaser}~(bottom left).
Meanwhile $\omega$ and $\kappa$ fall, and $\kappa$ reaches its minimum
(${\approx}4{\times}10^{-3}$, $\Delta\theta\approx 19^\circ$ per unit of depth)
exactly when $a_t$ crosses zero at mid-horizon $t{=}4$: the path is at its
straightest while features of different classes are pushed apart.
That single tangent direction still carries on average $\approx 27\%$ of
$\|a\|$, a large share in a $\sim\!70{,}000$-dimensional state
($384{\times}14{\times}14$); the rest is $a_n>0$, so the trajectory never
becomes purely one-dimensional.

After $t{=}4$ the same geometry returns as braking rather than search:
$a_t$ is negative (most so near $t{\approx}5.3$), $\|v\|$ decreases, and
$\omega$ and $\kappa$ rebound (secondary peaks near $t{=}6$--$6.5$).
Total $\|a\|$ declines overall (${\approx}34\to 8$) with a rebound while the
field brakes.

At $t{=}9$, $\|a\|$ is small, $\|v\|$ remains finite, and both $\omega$
and $\kappa$ are low but non-zero (${\approx}0.20\,\mathrm{rad}/t$,
${\approx}4.8{\times}10^{-3}$).
This is a coasting state, not an identity map: features keep drifting, the
regime of \cref{sec:beyond_horizon}.

Therefore the shared stage can be read as three jobs in one field.
At the start the model searches for a heading: $\|v\|$ is still moderate, so
the steps are small, while $\omega$ and $\kappa$ are high and the tangent turns
by tens of degrees per unit of depth.
It then accelerates along that heading more nearly in a straight line
($a_t>0$, $\omega$ and $\kappa$ fall, $\|v\|$ peaks): this is the expansion of
class features.
Finally it brakes and turns again with small steps ($a_t<0$, $\|v\|$ drops,
$\omega$ and $\kappa$ rebound)---a refinement of the same geometry as the
search, not a new block.
Over the $500$ images this profile is typical; $\|v\|$ varies most in the
expansion phase, where classes separate along different directions.

\subsubsection{Trajectories of untied ConvNeXt}
\label{sec:untied-trajectory-shape}
The untied ConvNeXt-T has no such arc (\cref{fig:kinematics-compare}, bottom).
Each block is a similar one-step map; the next block replaces the vector field
and sends the state into a different direction.
ResNet and Swin look the same.
At every integer, $v$ jumps, so finite-difference $\omega$, $a$ and
$\kappa$ diverge as $\Delta t\!\to\!0$.
Those inter-block pairs are omitted from the figure.
The reported $\omega{\sim}130$--$150\,\mathrm{rad}/t$ is not a turn of a
hundred radians: it is $\arccos\langle\hat v_d,\hat v_{d+1}\rangle/\Delta t$ on
a \emph{single} micro-step of $\Delta t{=}0.01$, so the heading jump between
blocks is $\omega\Delta t\approx 1.3$--$1.5\,\mathrm{rad}$
($\approx 75^\circ$--$86^\circ$).
Dividing a finite angle by a vanishing step is why the spikes explode; they
only record that neighbouring blocks do not share a direction.
What remains is the within-block motion, which is the fair comparison to the
shared flow.

Inside a block the dynamics are short.
$\|v\|$ is set by the new field and then decays slightly ($a_t<0$ on most of
the interval; a small positive tail appears only in the last blocks).
The envelope of $\|v\|$ grows in steps (${\approx}17$ after the first block,
${\approx}112$ at the start of the last), not as a bell.
After each switch, $\omega$ starts high (${\approx}0.6$--$0.9\,\mathrm{rad}/t$)
and falls toward ${\approx}0.45$--$0.55$: a brief reorientation, then a more
stable heading.
As in the shared model $a_n$ dominates (${\approx}97\%$ of $\|a\|$), so the
segment still bends, but there is no stage-wide accelerate-then-brake cycle.

That bending is not a hairpin and not a straight line.
Early intra-block $\kappa{\approx}4{\times}10^{-2}$ with $\|v\|{\approx}17$ is
\emph{larger} than the shared peak, again about $40^\circ$ of heading per unit
of depth; by the last block $\kappa$ has fallen to
${\approx}5{\times}10^{-3}$ (still $\sim 30^\circ$ per block, because $\|v\|$ is
larger), matching the shared \emph{minimum}.
Relative to the $80^\circ$ kinks at the integers the segments look nearly
linear; relative to a Euclidean straight line they still turn tens of degrees.

Therefore the picture to keep in mind is not a semicircle and not the shared
three-phase arc.
It is a chain of nine similar strokes, each pointing a different way.
Within a stroke the heading changes by a comparable angle
($\sim 30^\circ$--$40^\circ$): most of the turn sits at the start of the block,
where $\omega$ is high, and then the path flattens---a shallow arc, closer to a
stretched parabolic segment than to a circular quarter.
Because $\|v\|$ grows with depth, later strokes have the same order of heading
change but a longer arc: the pieces get larger, not more tightly wound.
At every integer those arcs are joined by an $\approx 80^\circ$ kink, where the
next block replaces the field.
The refined path is that polyline, not a search--expand--refine flow over
$T{=}9$.
That geometry is what \cref{sec:overparametrized_undoing} measures as
consecutive-block cosine $0.17$ and undo fraction $0.25$.
A deeper untied stack need not grow a shared-style flow: independent residual
blocks already behave like ensembles of short paths
\citep{veit2016residualnetworksbehavelike}.

\Cref{fig:dist-r1-compare} repeats the comparison of \cref{fig:dist-r1} for the
weight-tied network.
Both models start the same way: after the first native step the cosine to the $R{=}1$
path is $0.99$.
They then diverge.
In the standard ConvNeXt-T the block-end cosine keeps falling, to $0.93$ at $t{=}9$:
each block receives an input that has already drifted and adds its own discretisation
error on top.
In the weight-tied model it settles at $\approx 0.98$ after two or three steps and stays
there for the rest of the stage, so a single autonomous field keeps the refinement error
bounded instead of accumulating it.
The norms also behave differently: in ConvNeXt-T they nearly coincide ($203$ vs.\ $210$),
whereas the weight-tied refined path ends about $7\%$ shorter ($427$ vs.\ $458$), so part
of its smaller gap is radial.

\subsubsection{Integrating beyond the trained horizon}
\label{sec:beyond_horizon}
% TODO: plots for T >> 9: ||v|| plateau, a -> 0, kappa flat, x rectilinear.
At the end of the trained horizon the shared field has small acceleration but
non-zero velocity (\cref{sec:tied-trajectory-shape}).
Integrating past $T{=}9$ therefore enters an approximately uniform rectilinear
regime: $a$ and $\kappa$ flatten, $\|v\|$ stays roughly constant, and $x$ drifts
indefinitely along a fixed direction.
That continuation leaves the domain that stage~4 and the classification head were
trained on, and predictions collapse, as in the overthinking of recurrent networks
run past their training depth~\citep{bansal2022endtoend}.
If instead the block at $T{=}9$ were the identity ($v{=}0$), the state would sit
still and one could in principle integrate indefinitely without that drift, as in
deep equilibrium models that solve directly for such a fixed point~\citep{bai2019deep};
the trained shared field does not realise that fixed point.

The untied ConvNeXt, Swin and ResNet behave differently: with $R{=}100$ and Euler
step~$1$ (blocks never trained for reuse) the dynamics resemble a coarse version
of the $R{=}100$, $\Delta t{=}0.01$ refinement---larger magnitude, still tied to
block boundaries rather than a single coasting field.
