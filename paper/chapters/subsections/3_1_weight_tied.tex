\subsection{Do weight-tied stage-3 networks achieve similar results?}
\label{sec:weight_tied}

We first ask whether the stage-3 transport needs nine independent blocks, or whether
one shared block applied nine times is enough.
Sharing weights across depth is not new: residual networks with shared blocks have been
linked to recurrent networks~\citep{liao2016bridging}, residual networks with partially
shared blocks have been trained as compact CNNs~\citep{sharesnet,savarese2019learning},
and looped or universal transformers reuse a single layer or a fixed block of layers
many times~\citep{dehghani2019universal,giannou2023looped}.
\citet{sun2025painters} study a related question in pretrained transformers: they skip,
reorder, parallelise and repeat middle layers, and find that the middle layers share a
representation space but do not implement the same operation---replacing them by
repeated copies of the central layer is the most damaging intervention they test.
Large language models likewise tolerate deleting or swapping middle
layers~\citep{lad2025robustness} and pruning a large fraction of the deepest
ones after light fine-tuning~\citep{gromov2024unreasonable}.
Analogous ablations on ConvNeXt stage~3 (permuting, duplicating or substituting
blocks) are reported in \cref{app:block-ops}.
Reusing blocks that were trained to be different tests the wrong object, however; we
instead ask whether a stage \emph{trained} to use a single shared function reaches a
similar accuracy level.

We train a vanilla ConvNeXt-T and a weight-tied stage-3 variant under the same recipe
(ImageNet-1k, batch size $1024$; configuration in \cref{app:training}).
Our baseline reaches 80.97\% top-1 (28.59M params), slightly below the original
ConvNeXt-T report of 82.1\%~\citep{liu2022convnext}---expected under our hardware
constraints---but all comparisons below use models trained by us under matched
conditions.

The weight-tied model (18.97M params, 33\% fewer) reaches 80.36\% top-1
($-0.61$ points).
Sharing all four stages (8.68M params, 70\% fewer) reaches 79.01\%
($-1.96$ points).

A network that implements (approximately) the same residual function at every depth
can therefore sit close to the untied baseline.
That motivates the next subsection---not as a recipe for a deeper or larger network,
but as an inference-time probe: if stage~3 is (approximately) an Euler discretisation,
refining it with step $\Delta t{=}1/R$ at fixed $T{=}9$ should keep a decoder-usable
state and, equally important, yield high-resolution trajectories for analysis.
We apply that probe to the weight-tied model and to standard residual architectures.
