\label{sec:indefinite_looping}

Write the residual of block $i$ as $f_i(x)=\mathrm{block}_i(x)-x$.
Over a stage of $n$ blocks starting from the output $x_0$ of the previous stage, the
native network takes one Euler step of size $1$ per block,
\begin{equation}
    x_{i+1} \;=\; x_i + f_i(x_i),
    \qquad
    x_n \;=\; x_0 + \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} f_i(x_i).
    \label{eq:euler-stage}
\end{equation}
We read each block as a field integrated over its own unit of depth, so that the
continuous object behind~\eqref{eq:euler-stage} is
\begin{equation}
    x_{i+1} \;=\; x_i + \int_{i}^{i+1} f_i\bigl(x(t)\bigr)\,dt,
    \qquad T \;=\; n,
    \label{eq:sum-of-integrals}
\end{equation}
a switched system in the control sense~\citep{liberzon2003switching}, with the
piecewise-constant schedule $\theta_t=\theta_{\lfloor t\rfloor}$.
In a weight-tied stage $f_i\equiv f$ and~\eqref{eq:sum-of-integrals} collapses to a
single autonomous ODE,
\begin{equation}
    x_T \;=\; x_0 + \int_{0}^{T} f\bigl(x(t)\bigr)\,dt,
    \qquad T \;=\; n.
    \label{eq:weight-tied-ode}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Repetitions $R$.}
We refine with one parameter $R$: each block is applied $R$ times with step
$\Delta t{=}1/R$, so that $T{=}nR\,\Delta t{=}n$ is kept and only the resolution
changes,
\begin{equation}
    x_T \;=\; x_0 + \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} \sum_{r=0}^{R-1} \Delta t\, f_i\bigl(x_{i + r\Delta t}\bigr).
    \label{eq:refined-stage}
\end{equation}
For a weight-tied stage $R$ means $nR$ applications of the shared block.
$R{=}1$ recovers the native network; as $R$ grows the scheme approaches
\eqref{eq:sum-of-integrals}, and we also compare Midpoint (RK2) and RK4 on the same
horizon.
Nothing is retrained.

\paragraph{The continuous limit stays usable.}
We refine our ConvNeXt-T and weight-tied ConvNeXt-T, and the torchvision Swin-T
(\texttt{IMAGENET1K\_V1}), ResNet-50 and ResNet-101 (\texttt{IMAGENET1K\_V2})
(\cref{fig:refinement}, left; \cref{tab:r-ablation}).
All models are evaluated on the full validation set with one transform (resize to $256$,
bilinear, centre crop $224$), so the torchvision models score slightly differently from
their reported $81.47$, $80.86$ and $81.89\%$, which use their own resize.
Accuracy decreases monotonically with $R$ and plateaus at a limit that Euler,
Midpoint and RK4 share once the step is fine enough (\cref{app:solvers}); the
endpoints themselves agree to within $0.25^\circ$ (\cref{app:solver-endpoints}).
The limit costs $0.62$ to $0.91$ points on the untied models and $0.22$ on the
weight-tied one.
The native network is the best point of every sweep and is \emph{not} at the limit:
RK4 with the nine native unit steps already lands on it.
The decoder is tuned to the state produced by one Euler step of size $1$, not to the
flow that step approximates.

\paragraph{The horizon matters, the resolution does not.}
Repeating blocks without shrinking the step ($R{=}10$ or $100$ with $\Delta t{=}1$)
collapses every model to $0.10$--$1.03\%$ top-1, and the weight-tied network collapses
in the same way when $T$ leaves the trained horizon (\cref{app:solvers}), as recurrent
networks iterated past their training depth do~\citep{bansal2022endtoend}.
Even $R{=}3$ with $\Delta t{=}1$ ($T{=}27$) drops ConvNeXt-T to $41.0\%$.
With large steps at $T{=}9$ the tied model needs a higher-order solver: with $4$
steps, RK4 reaches $78.9\%$ and Euler $67.8\%$.

\paragraph{The limit is not the native state.}
Near-baseline accuracy does not mean that $R$ steps of size $1/R$ reproduce the unit
step.
On ConvNeXt-T the refined endpoint ($R{=}100$) sits $21^\circ$ from the native one,
with a relative distance of $0.36$ while the norms differ by $3\%$; on the weight-tied
model the gap is $11^\circ$ and $0.20$ (\cref{app:solver-endpoints}).
The untied gap accumulates block after block, whereas the tied one stops growing after
two or three steps.
Stage~4 and the head nevertheless classify the refined state at a small cost.

\paragraph{Adapting the decoder.}
Since the gap sits in the decoder's input, we finetune only stage~4 and the head on
the refined transport, with stem and stages~1--3 frozen.
On the weight-tied model at $128$ steps ($\Delta t{=}9/128$), $50$ epochs with a cosine
schedule from $10^{-6}$ to $10^{-8}$ raise top-1 from $80.14\%$ to $80.33\%$, the
native level (\cref{app:training}).

\paragraph{Smooth schedules.}
Replacing the piecewise-constant schedule by a bilinear blend of consecutive block
weights costs $6.6$ to $23.3$ points on every architecture (\cref{app:bilinear}): each
trained block is meant for its own unit of depth.
