\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Refining residual stages at a fixed horizon is a training-free probe: on ConvNeXt,
Swin and ResNet the continuous limit costs less than \refineLossBound{} top-1 point,
whereas changing the horizon collapses every network, and the refined path stays
faithful to the native Gram geometry.
A trained stage is a switched composition of one ODE per block, not one ODE with
weights smooth in depth.
In ConvNeXt-T the nine blocks write nearly orthogonal, positively correlated
directions that no later block reverses, with straightness $\strUntied$ and turns of
about $\turnMean^\circ$ at every boundary.
Weight tying turns the same stage into one autonomous, anytime flow that a frozen
decoder reads much earlier, at a cost of $\accGapTied$ points that extra depth does
not buy.
The open design question is how to keep most of that autonomy while allowing a
controlled dependence on depth.
