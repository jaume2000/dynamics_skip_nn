\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The weight-tied stage~3 was introduced as a control: the simplest residual stage
whose refinement is a genuine autonomous ODE.
It ended up as the other half of the argument.
Read side by side, the two models show that the same residual algebra $x+f(x)$
supports two different transports, and that the difference explains most of what
nine independent blocks buy.
\Cref{tab:headline} collects the numbers that carry this reading.

\paragraph{Why one shared block is enough.}
One shared residual map, applied nine times, carries almost all of the accuracy of
an untied ConvNeXt-T stage~3 ($-0.61$ top-1 with $33\%$ fewer parameters), and
sharing every stage still reaches $79.01\%$ with $70\%$ fewer.
We read this as a consequence of how the shared block is trained.
Because it is applied at every depth, it must act sensibly on every state it
visits, so it cannot defer work to a later block that does not exist.
The prefix probe shows this directly (\cref{fig:prefix-acc}): decoding with the
frozen stage~4 and head, the tied path already reaches $39.5\%$ at $t{=}1$ and
$74.7\%$ at $t{=}3$, against $10.7\%$ and $30.6\%$ for the untied stack; half of
the net displacement is done by $t{\approx}4.1$ instead of $7.2$ (\cref{tab:undo}).
The kinematics agree (\cref{fig:kinematics-compare}).
The untied stack accelerates toward the end ($\|v\|$ grows from ${\approx}17$ to
${\approx}112$ across blocks), whereas the shared field makes its largest step in
mid-stage and spends the last third braking and turning, i.e.\ refining.
This is close to the iterative-refinement reading of residual stages
\citep{greff2017highway,jastrzebski2018iterative} and to the observation that a
weight-shared ResNet behaves as a recurrent network at little cost
\citep{liao2016bridging}; our contribution is to show what that refinement looks
like as a trajectory.

\paragraph{The untied stack spends depth undoing itself.}
The nine independent blocks do not implement nine steps of one transport.
Almost half of their per-coordinate travel cancels before $t{=}9$ (wasted motion
$0.48$, undo $0.25$), and consecutive block displacements are nearly orthogonal
(cosine $0.17$); the shared field barely cancels (undo $0.02$, cosine $0.92$).
This redundancy is already present in the native network: the $R{=}1$ and
$R{=}100$ statistics of ConvNeXt-T are identical.
What refinement adds is \emph{where} it happens.
At $R{=}100$, $45\%$ of all sign flips fall on the eight block boundaries, which
are under $1\%$ of the transitions, a concentration of about fifty times.
Within a block the path is a shallow arc that turns $30^\circ$--$40^\circ$; at the
integers it turns by $\approx 80^\circ$ as the next block replaces the field
(\cref{sec:untied-trajectory-shape}).
The redundancy is therefore a property of switching the field, not of the
integration inside each block.

\paragraph{A kinematic reading of the stage.}
These trajectories argue against reading a residual stage as a sequence of new
representations---a unit that detects ``a wheel'' in one block and ``a car'' in
the next.
Within a stage the representation is transported, not replaced: the same block can
be applied a hundred times at $\Delta t{=}0.01$ and the decoder still reads the
result.
A more useful picture follows from the manifold hypothesis
\citep{bengio2013representation}: natural images lie near a low-dimensional
manifold, and the stage deforms its image under the encoder into an output
manifold where classes are separable, in line with the untangling view of
object recognition \citep{dicarlo2007untangling} and with geometric and
topological analyses of deep representations
\citep{brahma2016why,hauser2017principles,naitzat2020topology,cohen2020separability}.
In the weight-tied model that deformation is smooth: the state searches for a
heading, expands along it, and then refines (\cref{sec:tied-trajectory-shape}).
In the untied stack it proceeds in jolts: each block bends the state locally,
stops, and the next one pushes elsewhere, with increasing speed.
Since the same field acts on every input, the $500$ trajectories we integrate are a
finite sample of how the whole manifold is transported.
What we do not measure are properties of the manifold itself, such as its intrinsic
dimension or the separability of classes along $t$; a sample of one image per class
is too sparse for that, and we leave it to future work.

\paragraph{Refined and native paths are displaced but equivalent.}
Every architecture we tested converges to a single continuous limit once the
horizon $T$ is held fixed, and that limit keeps the function (gaps $0.2$--$0.9$
points).
The native unit-step path is not that limit, and the gap is not small in
geometric terms.
On ConvNeXt-T the refined endpoint is $21^\circ$ away from the native state, a
relative distance of $0.36$; the whole stage rotates the state by only
${\approx}78^\circ$ from $x_0$ to $x_9$.
On the weight-tied model the drift saturates at $11^\circ$ (relative distance
$0.20$; \cref{sec:refinement_error}).
Yet top-1 moves by $0.7$ and $0.2$ points.
The decoder is thus nearly invariant to large displacements along the directions
that refinement explores, which is consistent with a light adaptation of stage~4
and the head restoring the baseline at $R{=}3$ ($80.93\%$).
What the decoder does depend on is the integration time: breaking the horizon
($R$ repeats at $\Delta t{=}1$) collapses every network to chance, and integrating
the shared field past $T$ drifts out of the decoder's domain
(\cref{sec:beyond_horizon}).
A trained stage is therefore a sum of one-step Euler solves, each replaceable by a
finer integral of the \emph{same} block, but not a Neural ODE whose parameters vary
smoothly with depth: bilinear interpolation of consecutive $\theta_k$ costs
$6$--$23$ points (\cref{app:bilinear}).
A random residual field also has a continuous limit (\cref{app:random-init}); what
training adds is that the limit remains a classifier.

\paragraph{Refinement as a probe.}
Refining at a fixed horizon requires no retraining and turns any residual stage
into a trajectory sampled as finely as needed.
The weight-tied model is the clearest case, because its refined path is continuous
over the whole stage, but the same procedure separates within-block motion from
block switches in untied networks.
Nothing in the probe is specific to ConvNeXt.
We already apply the refinement to Swin-T and ResNet-50/101
(\cref{sec:indefinite_looping}, \cref{app:solvers,app:random-init,app:massive-activations}),
whose limits behave like ConvNeXt's, although the kinematic analysis of
\cref{sec:geometrical_analysis} is reported for ConvNeXt only.
The velocity decomposition, curvature, undo statistics and prefix decoding apply to
any residual stream.
In language models the prefix probe is the logit or tuned lens
\citep{belrose2023tuned}, trajectory geometry across layers is starting to be
measured \citep{pandey2026trajectorygeometrytransformerrepresentations}, and
looped or universal transformers \citep{dehghani2019universal,giannou2023looped}
are the weight-tied counterpart of our shared stage.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
All results are on ImageNet-1k classification, and the trained comparison between
tied and untied stages uses one architecture, ConvNeXt-T.
The trajectory metrics exclude the massive ConvNeXt channel and the Swin corner
tokens (\cref{sec:massive_activations}); they describe the remaining state, and the
role of the massive channel as a depth signal remains a conjecture.
The prefix probe uses a decoder trained only on $x_T$, so low mid-stage accuracy
means the state has not yet entered the decoder's operating region, not that it
lacks information.
Finally, we track a sample of the manifold rather than its geometry; pairwise
distances or class separability along $t$, on many images per class, would make
that reading quantitative.

\paragraph{Toward more expressivity per parameter.}
The leftover $0.6$ points are what independent $\theta_k$ still specialise, but the
untied stack pays for them with a large share of cancelled motion.
An architecture that keeps every parameter pushing toward the same goal would
combine a mostly autonomous field with a controlled degree of non-autonomy.
Two directions follow from our results: small per-block corrections on top of a
shared block, regularised so that the field stays close to autonomous, and cycles
of a few shared blocks instead of one.
Related ideas exist for convolutional networks \citep{savarese2019learning} and
transformers, through cyclic layer sharing \citep{takase2021lessons} and shared
layers with layer-wise low-rank updates \citep{bae2024relaxed}.
Designing and training such an architecture is beyond the scope of this work.
