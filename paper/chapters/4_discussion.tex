One shared residual map, applied nine times, already carries almost all of the
accuracy of an untied ConvNeXt-T stage~3 ($-0.61$ top-1; the sweep checkpoint in
\cref{tab:headline} is $80.34$ vs.\ $80.95$).
Sharing every stage still reaches $79.01$ with $70\%$ fewer parameters.
That is possible because the nine independent blocks do not implement nine steps of
one transport.
Almost half of their travel cancels before $t{=}9$ (undo $0.25$, wasted motion $0.48$),
consecutive displacements are nearly orthogonal (cosine $0.17$), and nearly half of
the sign flips sit on block boundaries---the switches of
$\theta(t)=\theta_{\lfloor t\rfloor}$.
A single autonomous field cannot undo itself in that way (undo $0.02$, cosine $0.92$,
straightness $0.78$).
The leftover six tenths of a point are then the part that independent $\theta_k$ still
specialise, not evidence of a different dynamical mechanism
(\cref{sec:overparametrized_undoing}).
Closing that gap with small per-block corrections on top of the shared field is the
content of \cref{sec:deltas}.

The same contrast appears when the discretisation is refined at inference, with no
training.
Every architecture we tested---tied or not---converges to a single continuous limit
once $T$ is held fixed, and that limit keeps the network's function (gaps $0.2$--$0.9$
points).
A random residual field has a continuous limit too (\cref{app:random-init}); what
training adds is that the limit remains a classifier.
The native unit-step path is \emph{not} that limit.
On ConvNeXt-T the refined endpoint sits $\approx 21^\circ$ from the $R{=}1$ state and
the mismatch accumulates at every block; on the weight-tied model it saturates at
$\approx 11^\circ$ (\cref{sec:refinement_error}).
Breaking the horizon ($R$ repeats at $\Delta t{=}1$) collapses every network to chance.
The invariant the head expects is the integration time $T{=}n$, not the number of
residual applications.
In that sense a trained stage is a sum of one-step Euler solves, each accurate enough
for the decoder at $\Delta t{=}1$ and each replaceable by a finer integral of the
\emph{same} block, but not a Neural ODE whose parameters vary smoothly with depth:
bilinear interpolation of consecutive $\theta_k$ costs $6$--$23$ points
(\cref{app:bilinear}).

Two regimes therefore sit under the same residual algebra $x+f(x)$.
Reuse one block throughout the stage and the field is autonomous: speed rises and
falls as one curve, refinement error stays bounded, features advance mostly
monotonically.
Use a new block at every integer and the field is switched: piecewise autonomous
inside each unit interval, discontinuous at the integers, with a sawtooth residual
norm and systematic undoing between neighbours
(\cref{fig:teaser,fig:refinement,fig:pooled-dynamics}).
That is the reading we take into the geometry of \cref{sec:geometrical_analysis}.
A light adaptation of stage~4 and the head already restores the baseline at $R{=}3$
($80.93\%$), which is consistent with the transport changing while remaining readable;
whether the same holds at the fine step of \cref{fig:dist-r1} is the remaining
decoder check.
Whether one can retain the expressivity of independent $\theta_k$ while suppressing
cancellation---and thus get more accuracy per parameter---is left for future work.
